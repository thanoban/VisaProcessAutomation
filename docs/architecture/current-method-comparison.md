# Current Method Comparison

## What the existing Sri Lanka method already does

- official ETA channel for short visits
- category separation across Tourist, Business, and Transit
- extension services through official immigration channels
- head-office and appointment handling where needed
- officer or border-side discretion remains in place

## Where the current method appears weaker operationally

### Fragmented status understanding

ETA status, border clearance, extension status, and manual review are not naturally visible as one timeline to the applicant or officer.

### Rule-publication inconsistency risk

Public-facing channels may not always reflect a clean single operational truth at the same time.

### Exception handling is harder to see

Restricted-nationality, sponsor, or manual-review cases can become process friction instead of structured workflow states.

### Applicant confusion risk

Applicants can be uncertain about:

- whether ETA is enough by itself
- what proof to carry
- when they must re-upload documents
- how extension and appointment paths work

### Supervisor visibility appears limited

Operational staff need better visibility into:

- pending cases
- exception queues
- document defect trends
- override rates
- rule-change fallout

## How VisaFlow should be better

### Better than today for applicants

- one case timeline
- clear next action
- explicit action owner
- clearer re-upload and exception messaging

### Better than today for officers

- unified case facts
- normalized evidence summary
- policy and rule version visibility
- clearer manual-referral reason

### Better than today for supervisors

- queue-state visibility
- extension bottleneck visibility
- unreadable upload metrics
- override and stuck-case analytics

### Better than today for governance

- explicit circular and policy versioning
- auditable rule selection
- cleaner reconstruction of what the system recommended and why
